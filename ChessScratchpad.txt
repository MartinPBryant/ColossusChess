I've been exploring more materially imbalance positions to try to identify a pattern that might help us isolate the problem.

Using the standard chess starting position I edited the board to remove various back rank pieces leaving the pawns on their initial squares to rule out any possible tactics.

In this position...
4k3/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w - - 0 1
I have removed all of black's back rank pieces leaving its pawns.
Using the standard recognised values for the pieces (queen=9, etc) this gives white a material advantage of 31 pawns.
The network evaluation with white to move is 30.150152 which makes sense. Also it demonstrates that the network can extrapolate scores for material imbalances greater than the +/-10 it was trained on.
The network evaluation with black to move is -3.856020 which is obviously wrong.
I reflected and colour swapped the position to this...
rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/4K3 b - - 0 1
Now the evaluation with black to move is 30.150152 (correct) and with white to move is -3.856020 (wrong).
I think this shows that the issue is not colour related (i.e. anything to do with white/black) but side-to-move related.

Further I tried other material imbalances.
I noticed that the network was roughly correct if the material imbalance was smaller than about +/-10 pawns but got progressively worse the higher the material imbalance became.
Also the wrong values seemed less erroneous if the material imbalance was comprised of a single piece plus many pawns even beyond the +/-10 barrier
e.g. in this position...
4k3/8/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/3QK3 w - - 0 1
white is ahead by a queen and 8 pawns for a material imbalance of 17.
The network gives 18.272493 for white to move and -14.218922 for black to move.
But in this position...
4k3/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RN1QK3 w - - 0 1
where white is a queen, rook and knight up (also 17 pawns up), the network gives 17.802629 for white to move and -7.296466 for black to move.

So i think there are two things we can identify here...
1. The network evaluates the position correctly if the side-to-move has the material advantage but wrongly if the side-to-move has the material deficit.
2. If the material imbalance is composed of pawns the network is much closer to being correct than if the material imbalance is composed of non-pawns.
